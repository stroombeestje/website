How to read this

Each text is tagged with where the words came from. I traced these through the repository history: for every text I walked back through every commit that touched it and found the one that actually wrote the words, ignoring later mechanical edits such as the em-dash sweep and the spelling fixes. The tag is that commit, so it is a trace and not a memory. Correct it where it is wrong.

AI = written by the assistant, mostly from the old site and what could be found online. This is what to check first.
FROM A DOCUMENT = written up from a real source, a press kit, an official text, a programme, a script, an article. The facts come from that document; the wording still is not yours.
YOUR WORDS = your own writing or dictation, kept as you gave it.

Where it stands

Of the 47 project texts: AI 41, from a document 3, your words 3. Your instinct is right, the great majority is not your writing.

Two things to know first

Every project also carries a second text called the excerpt, and the website never shows it. There are 47, one per project, and no page reads the field. They appear below only so you can see they exist. Either they should be deleted or given a job. Nothing written there today will appear on the site.

3. Points of Inaccessibility

Year: 2025 | Role: Real-time point-cloud visuals & cover artwork | Location: Uncloud residency, Utrecht | With: Rafael Anton Irisarri | Category: Performance

[FROM A DOCUMENT] 247 words, written 2026-08-12

Points of Inaccessibility: official press-kit text

LEFT COLUMN

Points of Inaccessibility is an immersive audiovisual performance by American composer Rafael Anton Irisarri and Dutch media artist Jaco Schilp, who met by chance in Mexico City in 2024.

The piece was developed during a residency at Uncloud in Utrecht in the spring of 2025, made possible through a grant from the K.F. Hein Fonds. A physical edition on BioVinyl is released by Black Knoll Editions, with worldwide distribution via Morr Music. The work has since been performed live internationally, including at Volumens Festival in Valencia.

RIGHT COLUMN, italic, the statement

The title draws from the geographic idea of poles of inaccessibility, those remote coordinates on Earth farthest from any boundary or point of entry. These are not places defined by what they are, but by what they lack: proximity, reach, connection. The work reimagines this idea for our digital era, mapping emotional and perceptual landscapes that feel similarly unreachable.

This haunting performance explores grief, loneliness, and mediated intimacy through the fragmented aesthetics of point cloud data. Shifting constellations of data points render landscapes, objects, and memories as ghostly, half-formed presences, always on the verge of dissolving. Digital spaces become both a refuge and a void, where flickering places, distorted voices, and fragile connections evoke the tension between presence and absence.

Blending emotionally charged soundscapes with ephemeral, dissolving visuals, Points of Inaccessibility asks: in a world where our interactions are mediated by screens and signals, can we ever truly connect? Or are we just isolated points, suspended in an infinite, unreachable space?

CREDITS
Music: Rafael Anton Irisarri. Real-time point-cloud visuals & artwork: Jaco Schilp. Artwork edition: Daniel Castrejón. Vocals (closing track): Karen Vogt. Released on Black Knoll Editions. Live photography: Jota Martínez (Volumens Festival, Valencia).

RIGHT COLUMN, italic, the statement

Transliminal refers to the temporary estrangement that takes place during the intermediate phase of a transformation, of which the final destination is unknown.

With this theme, Uncloud presents a metaphor for the current state of the world, in which we all experience estrangement from our old realities. This feeling creates a temporary reality forced on us by urgent experiences. More than ever we feel that our future is uncertain.

In this transliminal phase, we find ourselves entangled with a process of transformation where the distinction between the tangible and intangible is undefined; where the threshold between reality and speculation is sometimes perceived as unknown. The overwhelming uncertainty of this phase is created by the in-betweenness of the state we are in. At the same time, this state leads also to renewed experience, thoughts and insights. It leads into the creation of something new: transformation.

Within a transforming world, art can help us understand the processes we are in. By providing authentic and different experiences, art facilitates perspective, proposes possible solutions for modern challenges and composes alternative outcomes for the future.

Transliminal art is intrinsically connected to the context in which it is made and presented. It provides a re-interpretation of not only its direct environment, but also of the world it is surrounded by. Transliminal art finds itself in the now. Based on modern knowledge and equipped with contemporary technology, it encourages a radically different and experimental approach to modern tools and narratives. It often takes the form of an abstract multi-sensory experience, where the visitor forms their own narrative, reflection and interpretation.

Within the context of a former forensic psychiatric observation institute, Uncloud 2021: Transliminal presents the visitor a metaphor for the digitized, isolated and transitional world we currently live in, and brings this back into the tangible physical reality.

8. The Anonymous Project

Year: 2023 | Role: Video mapping, content design & light design | Location: Le Guess Who?, Utrecht | Category: Live Show

[YOUR WORDS] 238 words, written 2026-08-13

The Anonymous Project: rewrite info text in Jaco's words, update role and credits

LEFT COLUMN

For Le Guess Who? 2023 I was commissioned to build the visual work that video-mapped the cube: a five-sided projection map driven by five projectors, with three different kinds of video content moving across its faces.

The first was a live point cloud: a real-time 3D scan of the artist inside the cube. Three depth cameras stayed in there with them and streamed the performance out through TouchDesigner, so the room could watch what was happening within without ever seeing a face.

The second was my first work with AI-generated content. I trained a model to sit across the five sides of the cube.

The third was volumetric 3D, generated in TouchDesigner from LiDAR scans of forests and places around Utrecht.

Everything was played out live from Resolume and TouchDesigner. The light design was built to make the whole room immersive, and I ran it b2b with Marc Huizinga on two light desks at the same time.

RIGHT COLUMN, italic, the statement

The Anonymous Project is a stage where artists perform inside a white cube, remaining anonymous at all times: before, during and after the performance. Audiences meet the work free of reputation or expectation, focused purely on the sound.

So the cube had to show and conceal at once. What the audience saw was the performance rendered back onto its own walls as drifting points of light. The shape of someone playing, breathing with the music, never a face. Presence without identity.

CREDITS
Commissioned by Le Guess Who? 2023. Video mapping, content design, light design & live operation: Jaco Schilp. Light desks, b2b: Marc Huizinga. Photography: Ward Mevis. Technique: five-projector cube mapping, real-time point cloud from three depth cameras, a custom-trained AI model and volumetric 3D from LiDAR scans around Utrecht, live in TouchDesigner and Resolume.

13. Panorama Utrecht

Year: 2022–ongoing | Role: Design & creation | Location: Stationsplein, Utrecht Centraal · Uncloud | Category: Installation

[AI] 229 words, written 2026-08-13

Panorama Utrecht: drop the exact run dates

LEFT COLUMN

Panorama Utrecht stood on the Stationsplein at Utrecht Centraal in 2022, as part of the city's 900th anniversary. The sculpture was shown again at Uncloud in 2023 and is still travelling. It is looking for a permanent location.

The composition of points is an artistic interpretation of aerial LiDAR data of Utrecht. For every point, a laser was fired at the city from a scanner in the air, and the height of that measuring point was derived from how long the reflection took. The 3D measurement of Utrecht holds roughly 1.1 billion points and comes from the Actueel Hoogtebestand Nederland dataset.

The audio composition is built from field recordings collected by cycling and walking through the city, switching between recognisable daily city sounds and composed ones. The contrast between them stands for the warm home feeling of Utrecht and the fast modern transformation of the city.

RIGHT COLUMN, italic, the statement

The light sculpture Panorama Utrecht shows the city from a digital perspective: a panoramic view of small luminous points that make all the walls disappear.

Aerial scanners fly over the Netherlands to collect billions of height data points. This work stands at the focal point between that digital world and the tangible city. A 360-degree sculpture you step into, where a play of light and a composition of city sounds together open a new perspective. You look straight through the walls of Utrecht.

Construction: Sander Grooten, Edwin Schulte, Jaco Schilp, Silvana Hurtd. Technical support: Tjarco van Heese, Cris Mollee, Jelle Grutterink. 15. LiDAR Utrecht, physical NFT series

Year: 2022 | Role: Artist | Location: Creative Coding Utrecht · NAR Café der Kunsten, Utrecht | Category: Artwork

[AI] 225 words, written 2026-08-17

LiDAR Utrecht 60x60 as an empty page, pictures tomorrow

LEFT COLUMN

This is the second of the two LiDAR Utrecht series, following the earlier works at 60 by 60 cm. Each piece is 300×300mm and captures a single Utrecht location from a chosen perspective in 3D space. Up close the buildings dissolve into thousands of luminous points, and every wall becomes light. The first five works in the series are Tivoli Vredenburg (5.1), the Stadskantoor (5.2), the Neude library (5.3), the windmill on the Adelaarstraat (5.4), and the Nijverheid / Havenloods (5.6).

Presented as “physical NFTs”, each sculpture is paired with a digital token that registers it, exploring the boundary between digital and physical art, and the shifting role of accessibility and ownership in a digitalising world.

The series was first shown in Math Expressionism, an exhibition of work by the international community of creative coders, presented by Creative Coding Utrecht at NAR Café der Kunsten. Alongside the work the programme ran workshops and discussions on NFTs, where makers shared their experience of communities like Hic et Nunc and Artblocks, and the controversies around them.

RIGHT COLUMN, italic, the statement

On the interface between the physical world and cyberspace, I make art that brings the two universes together. The LiDAR series interprets raw aerial LiDAR data of Utrecht into physical objects: point clouds of the city's landmarks engraved by laser into wood and lit from within by LED, framed in aluminium.

CREDITS
Physical NFT series. Laser-cut wood, engraved LiDAR point cloud, LED light, aluminium frame, 300×300mm. Point clouds derived from aerial LiDAR data of Utrecht. Shown in Math Expressionism by Creative Coding Utrecht, at NAR Café der Kunsten in Utrecht, made possible with the support of the Creative Industries Fund NL, Gemeente Utrecht and TheElephantNL.

16. Aquí y Ahora

Year: 2024 | Role: Artist | Location: Arthouse Pani, Tequisquiapan, Mexico | Category: Artwork

[AI] 234 words, written 2026-08-17

Keep the point cloud series apart

LEFT COLUMN

A month long residency at Arthouse Pani in Tequisquiapan, with twelve artists from around the world, curated by Santiago Pani. The show that closed it was called Aquí y Ahora.

I scanned the plants of the high desert around the house and turned the point clouds into physical work. One series is laser cut into white painted wood and into black plexiglas, each piece set in a frame with a light behind it, so the cloud reads as depth rather than as a picture. A second series is acid etched into brass, aluminium and copper, and every bath in the acid was printed onto paper before the next one went in, so the plate and its own history hang side by side. Titles follow the plants: Nopal, Agave, Maguey, Suculenta.

Espíritu de Nopal, a laser engraved oil painting on panel of 150 by 120 cm, was made together with Daniel Martin.

RIGHT COLUMN, italic, the statement

The point cloud is a way of holding a thing as measurement rather than as image. In Mexico the subject changed and the method held. A nopal scanned at close range is a dense, bristling mass of coordinates, and cutting that mass into brass or into wood makes it a body again, something with an edge and a weight and a shadow. The acid does the last part of the work on its own, and I stopped it when it had said enough.

CREDITS
Laser cut and acid etched point cloud works: Jaco Schilp. Espíritu de Nopal made with Daniel Martin. Residency and exhibition at Arthouse Pani, Tequisquiapan, curated by Santiago Pani, 2024.

35. Lely's Droom

Year: 2025 | Role: 3D scan, point cloud projection, projection mapping & show control | Location: Oosterlanderkerk, Oosterland · Wieringen | With: LiGHT-UP Collective | Category: Installation

Lely's Droom: name Jaco Schilp among the makers

LEFT COLUMN

Lely's Droom is a fifteen minute immersive experience in the Oosterlanderkerk in Oosterland on Wieringen, made by LiGHT-UP Collective, Jan Willem Campmans and Jaco Schilp for Erfgoedspektakel and shown through May 2025, with Tristan Gieler on the content. The church stands a short distance from the Afsluitdijk, which is the subject of the piece.

It starts from rediscovered sound recordings that Cornelis Lely made in May 1925, exactly a hundred years before the piece was shown. He speaks to whoever is still interested in his dream a century later, appearing in the church as a life size animated figure, voiced by Rik Rikken. Projection and light carry his story: the first plans from 1848, the years of rejection, the flood of January 1916 that killed twenty one people on land and thirty two more at sea, the Zuiderzeewet of 1918, the Amsteldiepdijk built to learn on, the Wieringen boulder clay that finally held, and the Afsluitdijk itself.

Lely died at his desk in January 1929 and never saw the work finished.

I made a full 3D scan of the church and projected it back onto its own geometry with my point cloud system in TouchDesigner, driving the points with displacement so the building itself starts to move like water. Between the shows the space held an idle state running on a soundscape I made for it.

RIGHT COLUMN, italic, the statement

Scanning the church and projecting it back onto itself means the building is both the screen and the subject. Push displacement into those points and the stone begins to move like water, which is the whole argument of the piece in one gesture: the land here is only ever borrowed from the sea.

Water is always moving, so waterworks are never finished. That is Lely's own line, and it is the reason the piece is addressed forward rather than back: he is not being commemorated, he is asking what we are doing with the problem now.

43. Universum 1.2

Year: 2018 | Role: 3D sculpture & video mapping | Location: Museum van Geluid | Category: Installation

[AI] 160 words, written 2026-08-14

Universum: lasers are triggers

LEFT COLUMN

Universum 1.2 is an interactive light sculpture made with Skander Jaïbi for Museum van Geluid.

Lasers in the space were triggered by where people stood. Those triggers drove the soundscape, and the soundscape triggered the visuals, so a visitor's movement travelled through sound before it ever arrived as light. The sphere hung from an electromotor and turned continuously, with the mapping built to stay locked to the shape as it moved.

RIGHT COLUMN, italic, the statement

An abstract planet, small enough to stand beside. Where you put yourself in the room changes how it behaves, so nobody meets quite the same planet, and nobody has it to themselves.

It opens a space between two worlds, the digital abstract one and the physical analogue one we live in. Holding them together makes a new micro universe where the two become one.

The work is part of an ongoing research into making the digital physically tangible, looking for a digital reality that can be shared with everyone.

CREDITS
Jaco Schilp: 3D sculpture and video mapping. Skander Jaïbi: interactive soundscape. Laser sensors trigger the soundscape, the soundscape triggers the visuals. Sphere mounted on a rotating electromotor, with the mapping tracking the turning shape. Presented at Museum van Geluid, 2018.

For Museum Night 2019 the whole LiGHT-UP team of that moment travelled to Nizhny Novgorod, Russia: Tim Walther, Lisa Derksen Castillo, Jan Willem Campmans and me.

We began with a light-drawing workshop for children, drawing with light on PlayStation uDraw tablets running Happytron, LiGHT-UP's own application for drawing with projected light.
